How to use Google Flow for text-to-video?

Howard Pong Yuen Lam · AI Notes · November 8, 2026 · 4 min read

The three Seedance notes in this series showed a tool where you write the detailed prompt. Google Flow works the other way round: you type one plain sentence, and Flow acts like a small film crew — it asks you a few questions, writes a shot-by-shot plan, and only then starts filming. This note follows a real example: a 30-second commercial for an imaginary company, Howard Orange Juice.

What you need before you start

  • A Google account that can open Google Flow. Access and pricing depend on your plan — each clip you generate costs credits, and Flow tells you the price before it starts. At the time of writing, a free account receives free credits that refill every day, and paid plans include monthly credits (see the official plans).
  • One sentence describing the video you want. That is all.

Watch the demo video

If the video does not load, open it in Google Drive: video (no account needed).

The video shows the whole process from this note: the one-sentence request, Flow’s questions, the six-frame storyboard, the one-word change to frame 5, the approvals — and the finished 30-second commercial.

Step 1. Start a new project and say what you want

Open Google Flow and start a new project. At the bottom of the screen there is a box labelled “What do you want to create?”. Type your one sentence: 

I want to create a 30 seconds commercial for Howard Orange Juice company.

No camera words, no style words, no structure — just the goal.

Step 2. Answer its questions

Flow does not start filming right away. It asks a few short questions first — here are the answers given in the demo video:

  • Overall tone? — bright and energetic.
  • Key moments to show? — fresh oranges being sliced.
  • A tagline? — we do not have one.

Answer in plain words and tell it to generate. If you have no tagline, Flow will happily invent one itself — in this example it came up with “Squeeze the day!”.

Step 3. Read the storyboard it writes

Flow now writes the plan a director would write: six frames of five seconds each, and for every frame it spells out the action, the camera, the lighting and the narration. The last frame of the example looked like this: 

Frame 6 (25-30s): The Hero Shot & Tagline
Action: The Howard Orange Juice bottle stands proudly on a clean marble
surface, flanked by fresh orange halves. 3D text sweeps onto the screen
below: "SQUEEZE THE DAY!"
Camera: Centered hero shot with a gentle push-in.
Lighting: Bright, pristine studio lighting with a soft orange back-glow.
Narration: "Howard Orange Juice. Squeeze the day."

Read the frames in order and check that the story makes sense before spending any credits.

Step 4. Change anything with words

If a frame is not what you imagined, you do not edit it by hand — you just tell Flow what to change. In the example, one instruction was: 

Change the man in frame 5 to Chinese.

Flow updates that frame and asks you to approve the change.

Step 5. Approve, and let it film

Now ask Flow to generate the video for all the frames. Before each clip it tells you the cost (“this 1 video generation, costing 10 credits”) and waits for you to press Approve — or Approve, do not ask again if you trust the plan. Each clip then joins a queue; at busy times Flow tells you it is “waiting in the queue due to high demand” and you simply wait. When every frame is done, the clips play together as one 30-second commercial.

Good to know

  • Credits are the budget. In the demo, talking to Flow and reading the storyboard cost nothing; only generating clips cost credits. Fix the plan first, film second.
  • There is a daily limit. Free credits do not last forever: you cannot generate too many clips in one day. If you run out, simply wait — the free credits come back tomorrow.
  • Flow says so itself: “Google Flow can make mistakes, so double check it.” Read each frame before approving.
  • Finishing touches come later. Once the clips exist, you can use other video tools to edit them, change the language, and add background music.
