How to use Seedance 2.5 for text-to-video (version 1)?

Howard Pong Yuen Lam · AI Notes · November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

Seedance 2.5 is ByteDance’s AI that turns written words into a short video. You type a description — called a prompt — and it makes the clip for you. No camera, no editing software, no filming skills needed. You can try it on ByteDance’s Jimeng website.

The whole skill is knowing what to write. This note shows the six-part formula behind every good Seedance prompt, a template you can copy and fill in, and one complete example. Write your prompt in English or Chinese — Seedance understands both.

What you need before you start

  • An account on Jimeng (ByteDance’s creative AI site, free to start).
  • One idea for a short clip — a person doing something, a place, a moment.

The core prompt formula

Every Seedance prompt is built from six parts. The first two are required; the other four are optional — add them only when you care about them. (Source: ByteDance’s Seedance 2.5 prompt guide and prompt examples.) 

Subject + Action or Event
+ Scene and Environment (optional)
+ Visual Style (optional)
+ Camera Movement/Cut (optional)
+ Audio (optional)
  • Subject + Action or Event — say who or what is doing what. This is the foundation of the video. Summarise the main action first; add detail only for the moments that matter, and do not describe the same action twice.
  • Scene and Environment — the location, time of day, weather, and what is around the subject.
  • Visual Style — the lighting, colours, materials and overall mood of the picture.
  • Camera Movement/Cut — the shot size (close-up, wide), the camera angle, how the camera moves, and any change of shot.
  • Audio — dialogue, voices, background sounds, sound effects and music.

A template you can copy

Replace each part in angle brackets with your own words. (Source: ByteDance’s Seedance 2.5 prompt guide and prompt examples.) 

<Subject> performs <primary action or event> in <scene and environment>.
The visuals feature <visual style>.
Use <shot size, camera angle, camera movement, or cuts>.
Audio includes <dialogue, ambience, sound effects, or music>.

Leave out a sentence completely if you do not need that part — a two-line prompt (subject and action only) is a perfectly good start.

A worked example

Here is a complete prompt that uses all six parts — a ceramic artist finishing a cup. It is the worked example from ByteDance’s own Seedance 2.5 materials. (Source: ByteDance’s Seedance 2.5 prompt guide and prompt examples.) 

A ceramic artist finishes a pale blue cup in a studio at dawn, lifts it
from the wheel, and places it in the center of a wooden shelf.
Soft morning light enters through the window. The wet clay has a delicate
sheen, and the workbench remains tidy.
Begin with a medium shot of the wheel-throwing process, slowly push in
toward the cup's surface texture, then cut to a frontal view of the shelf.
Retain the low hum of the pottery wheel, the friction of clay, and subtle
indoor ambience.

Line by line, this is just the formula with the blanks filled in:

  • Subject + Action — the ceramic artist finishes the cup, lifts it, places it on a shelf.
  • Scene — a studio at dawn, morning light through the window, a tidy workbench.
  • Visual style — soft light, the delicate sheen of wet clay.
  • Camera — a medium shot, a slow push-in, then a cut to a frontal view.
  • Audio — the hum of the wheel, the friction of clay, quiet room sound.

Good to know

  • Start small: write only the subject and action first, generate, and add more parts one at a time to see what each one changes.
  • One prompt makes one short clip. For a video with several steps (for example, packing an order from start to finish), you split the prompt into stages — that is the subject of the next note, How to use Seedance 2.5 for long videos (version 2)?
